Quantum

Quantum innovation in cancer diagnosis.

Cancer Innovate treats quantum computing as one of the tools — alongside artificial intelligence — for accelerating non-invasive early cancer screening.

Why it is on the agenda

Some questions in diagnosis are brutally computational: searching vast combinations of signals, modelling molecular behaviour, and sorting noise from something a clinician can act on. Quantum computing is being explored because those searches do not always fit comfortably on conventional machines.

The point is not the computer. The point is earlier, less invasive answers for patients, developed with researchers and clinicians rather than in isolation from care.

How this connects to the hub

Quantum work sits with the research centre and the wider portfolio. Teams who want to collaborate — academic groups, clinical partners, or technology builders — can start with a conversation.

A clear boundary

This page describes the direction Cancer Innovate has set in public. It is not a clinical claim, a trial result, or a promise that a quantum method is ready for patients today.

Questions, answered

Quantum & AI FAQ.

Is quantum computing being used on patients today?

No. Quantum methods are an exploration — a direction of travel for hard computational problems in diagnosis, not a clinical product in use.

Why pair quantum with AI?

AI is already finding earlier signals in data. Quantum is being explored for the classes of problems — huge search spaces and molecular simulation — where classical computing hits limits.

Who decides if a technology is worth pursuing?

Researchers, clinicians and patients together. A method only advances if it can survive real-world scrutiny.
